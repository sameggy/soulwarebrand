# MOOD BOARD 3: Photography Style & Technique

## Lighting, Composition, and Cinematics

### Cinematic Lighting References

**Films to Study for Lighting:**

- **Her (2013) - Warm Interior Scenes**
  - Cinematographer: Hoyte van Hoytema
  - What to capture: Warm, soft, natural-feeling light in futuristic spaces
  - Technique: Practical sources, warm color temperature, soft shadows
  - Apply to: All workshop interior scenes

- **Blade Runner 2049 (2017) - Interior Scenes**
  - Cinematographer: Roger Deakins
  - What to capture: Dramatic lighting with depth
  - Technique: Strong directional light, atmospheric haze, layered depth
  - Apply to: More dramatic "master craftsperson" shots
  - Note: Use warm scenes, not the cold dystopian ones

- **The Social Network (2010) - Office/Lab Scenes**
  - Cinematographer: Jeff Cronenweth
  - What to capture: Modern workspace lighting, focus on subjects
  - Technique: Practical monitors providing cool fill, warm key light
  - Apply to: Tech-heavy workshop scenes

- **Steve Jobs (2015) - Backstage/Work Scenes**
  - Cinematographer: Alwin H. Küchler
  - What to capture: Focused work environments, intimate moments
  - Technique: Natural window light, practical sources
  - Apply to: Individual craftsperson scenes

### Lighting Setups

**Setup 1: Natural Window Light (Daytime)**
- Large window as key light (camera left or right)
- Subject 3-6 feet from window
- White reflector opposite window for fill
- Warm white balance (3800-4200K)
- Soft, wrapping light on subject
- Background can have mix of warm overhead + cool screen glow
- Best for: Approachable, authentic, warm scenes

**Setup 2: Warm Overhead + Cool Accent (Studio)**
- Soft overhead key light (large softbox, warm)
- Cool "screen glow" from front/side (LED panel with cyan gel)
- Practical lamps in background (warm)
- Balanced white balance (4500-5000K), warm up in post
- Mix of warm and cool creates depth
- Best for: Tech-forward workshop scenes

**Setup 3: Dramatic Side Light (High Impact)**
- Strong directional light from side (window or large soft source)
- Minimal fill (natural bounce or subtle reflector)
- Dark background with practical lights
- Warm color temperature on subject, neutral/cool on background
- Creates sculpture and dimension
- Best for: "Master" shots, leadership, expertise

**Setup 4: Collaborative Bright Light (Team Scenes)**
- Bright, even overhead lighting (multiple sources)
- Still slightly warm (not fluorescent green)
- Minimal shadows, open and inviting
- Some practical sources visible (monitors, lamps)
- Energy and optimism
- Best for: Team collaboration, workshop overviews

### Composition Techniques

**Rule of Thirds:**
- Position subject on thirds, not dead center
- Eyes typically on upper third line
- Leave breathing room in direction of gaze
- Balance negative space

**Environmental Context:**
- Include workshop environment (not isolated)
- Use depth of field to keep subject sharp, environment slightly soft
- Aperture: f/2.8 - f/5.6 (sweet spot for environmental portraits)
- Show context without distraction

**Leading Lines:**
- Use workshop architecture (tables, shelves, walls)
- Draw eye to subject
- Create depth and dimension
- Diagonal lines add energy

**Layering:**
- Foreground element (slightly out of focus)
- Subject (sharp focus)
- Background (environmental context, soft)
- Creates depth in 2D medium

**Headroom & Framing:**
- Generous headroom for text overlay space
- Don't crowd subject to top of frame
- Allow for cropping variations (square, 16:9, vertical)

### Camera Settings & Specs

**Camera Body:**
- Full-frame sensor preferred (better low light, depth control)
- Minimum: APS-C sensor (still excellent)
- High resolution: 24MP+ for flexibility in post
- Shoot RAW (maximum editing flexibility)

**Lenses:**
- **35mm f/1.4 or f/2:** Environmental portraits, wider scenes, shows context
- **50mm f/1.4 or f/2:** Natural perspective, versatile, go-to lens
- **85mm f/1.4 or f/1.8:** Tighter portraits, beautiful compression and bokeh
- All focal lengths useful, bring all if possible

**Settings:**
- **Aperture:** f/2.8 - f/5.6 (environmental context with subject separation)
- **Shutter Speed:** 1/125s minimum (avoid motion blur), faster if subject moving
- **ISO:** Keep low (100-800), quality matters more than available light
- **White Balance:** Warm (3800-4500K) or custom for accurate color, refine in post

**File Format:**
- RAW (DNG, CR2, NEF depending on camera)
- Deliver final JPGs in sRGB for web, Adobe RGB for print
- Minimum 4000px width for hero images
- Various sizes for different applications

### Holographic UI Style Guide

**Visual Characteristics:**

**Structure:**
- Geometric shapes (rectangles, lines, circles, hexagons)
- Semi-transparent panels (60-80% opacity)
- Glowing edges (2-4px glow in Light Cyan #80E8FF)
- Frosted glass effect (subtle blur on background through UI)

**Colors:**
- Primary: Bright Cyan (#00D9FF)
- Secondary: True Blue (#0088FF)
- Depth/shadows: Deep Tech Blue (#004D99)
- Highlights: Light Cyan Glow (#80E8FF)
- Text on holograms: White or Light Cyan

**Typography on Holograms:**
- Font: Neue Haas Grotesk Light or Regular
- Size: Smaller than primary text (supporting role)
- Color: White (#FFFFFF) or Light Cyan Glow (#80E8FF)
- Monospace (JetBrains Mono) for code or technical data

**Animation Principles (If Animated):**
- Smooth, ease-in-out transitions (no linear)
- Write-in effect for text appearing
- Subtle pulse/glow (0.7-1.0 opacity, 2-3 second loop)
- Feels responsive to human interaction

**Content Types:**

**Data Visualizations:**
- Bar charts (vertical bars of light)
- Line graphs (glowing lines with nodes)
- Network diagrams (nodes and connections)
- Pie/donut charts (circular, semi-transparent segments)
- Always semi-transparent, never solid

**Dashboards:**
- Grid layouts (clean alignment)
- Metrics in panels (KPIs, numbers)
- Mini charts (sparklines, small visualizations)
- Status indicators (dots, icons)

**Technical Readouts:**
- System status displays
- Code snippets (monospace font)
- Technical specifications
- Process flows

**Interactive Elements:**
- Buttons (subtle, outlined style)
- Sliders and controls
- Toggle switches
- All appear touchable/interactable

### Creating Holographic Elements

**Software Options:**

**For Static Images (Photoshop/Illustrator):**
1. Design UI element in Illustrator
   - Vector shapes, clean and geometric
   - Use cyan color palette
   - Add glow effect (outer glow, light cyan)
2. Import to Photoshop on workshop photo layer
3. Apply blend mode: Screen or Lighten
4. Adjust opacity: 60-80%
5. Add Gaussian blur (1-2px) for slightly soft edge
6. Add reflection on nearby surfaces (subtle)

**For Motion Graphics (After Effects):**
1. Design UI in Illustrator or directly in AE
2. Animate with ease curves
3. Add glow effects (Glow plugin or manual)
4. Render with alpha channel
5. Composite into workshop footage
6. Match lighting and add reflections

**For 3D Holograms (Cinema 4D/Blender):**
1. Model 3D UI elements or data visualizations
2. Apply emissive materials (cyan/blue glow)
3. Render with transparency
4. Composite into photo with correct perspective
5. Add atmospheric integration

**Quick DIY Method:**
1. Create UI element in Canva or Figma
2. Export as PNG with transparency
3. Open in Photoshop with workshop photo
4. Set blend mode to Screen
5. Add layer style: Outer Glow (cyan color, 8-12px)
6. Reduce opacity to 70-80%
7. Apply slight blur if needed

### Integration Best Practices

**Lighting Integration:**
- Hologram should emit light onto nearby surfaces
- Add subtle cyan glow on person's face/hands if near hologram
- Surfaces reflect the holographic color slightly
- Shadows don't change (hologram is light source not solid object)

**Perspective Accuracy:**
- Match perspective of the workshop space
- Use Photoshop's perspective warp if needed
- Floating elements should follow room's depth
- Closer elements larger, further elements smaller

**Depth and Atmosphere:**
- Foreground holograms can be slightly out of focus
- Add atmospheric haze between camera and hologram (subtle)
- Layer multiple holographic elements at different depths
- Creates believable integration

**Scale and Proportion:**
- Human-scale (not too small or overwhelmingly large)
- Typically 12-36" wide for personal workspace displays
- Larger for collaborative/overview displays
- Must be legible but not dominant

**Content Legibility:**
- Text should be readable (if meant to be read)
- Charts should make sense (real data better than random)
- Don't overcomplicate with too much detail
- Viewer should understand what it is at a glance

### Color Grading Process

**Step 1: Color Correction (Make it Accurate)**
- Set proper white balance
- Adjust exposure (not too dark, not blown out)
- Balance highlights and shadows
- Ensure skin tones are natural
- Fix any color casts

**Step 2: Color Grading (Make it Beautiful)**
- **Shadows:** Add slight warm tone (orange/yellow)
- **Midtones:** Keep relatively neutral, slightly warm
- **Highlights:** Neutral to slightly warm (not cool)
- **Overall Temperature:** 3800-4500K feeling
- **Saturation:** Slight boost in warm tones (+5-10%)
- **Saturation:** Slight decrease in cool tones (-5-10%) except holograms

**Step 3: Atmospheric Enhancement**
- Add subtle haze/atmosphere (if needed)
- Enhance light rays from windows
- Create depth with color layers
- Cool tones recede, warm tones advance

**Step 4: Holographic Integration**
- Add holographic UI elements
- Ensure they emit light onto surroundings
- Balance so image remains warm-dominant
- Cyan holograms pop against warm environment

**Step 5: Final Polish**
- Gentle vignette (darken edges slightly)
- Sharpen subject (not over-sharpen)
- Noise reduction if needed
- Final contrast adjustment
